# FIN-BR-040 — Revenue, Cost of Goods Sold, and Margin Recognition

Purpose: Ensure sales-related financial performance is recognized once, at the correct lifecycle point, and from complete authoritative amounts.

Rules:
- Revenue recognition must be triggered only by the order or settlement state defined by the active sales and financial policy.
- Payment receipt, payment confirmation, shipment, completion, and settlement are distinct events and must not be treated as interchangeable unless the active policy explicitly defines them as equivalent for recognition.
- Every recognized sale must preserve at minimum:
  - Order ID;
  - buyer or sales channel;
  - recognition date;
  - item revenue;
  - discounts;
  - additional charges;
  - buyer shipping charged;
  - marketplace or payment fees;
  - taxes;
  - actual shipping cost;
  - cost of goods sold;
  - other approved direct costs;
  - resulting gross margin or order profit;
  - currency;
  - ownership and profit-distribution snapshot.
- Revenue must be recognized from the amount economically earned and must not include deposits, unidentified funds, refundable advances, or amounts collected on behalf of another party unless policy requires gross presentation.
- Cost of goods sold must use the authoritative cost basis of the inventory actually sold and must be recognized exactly once.
- Shipping surplus or deficit must follow the active sales rule and must remain separately traceable even when included in order profit.
- Marketplace fees, payment fees, discounts, taxes, rebates, and additional charges must be classified consistently and must not be hidden through net balance adjustments.
- Profit distribution must be calculated only after all required revenue, cost, fee, shipping, tax, discount, refund, and adjustment components for the recognition event are resolved or explicitly accrued.
- A completed sale must not generate distributable profit when the calculated result is a loss; loss allocation must follow the applicable ownership and financial rules.
- Partial fulfillment, partial cancellation, partial refund, or split settlement must recognize only the affected portion and preserve the remaining open amount.
- A refund, chargeback, cancellation, return, or post-completion adjustment must use linked reversing or adjusting effects and must not overwrite the original recognized sale.
- Revenue and cost recognition must remain linked to inventory status transitions and must detect mismatches such as sold inventory without cost recognition or recognized cost without a valid sale.
- Manual profit entries that bypass order economics, cost basis, and distribution snapshots are prohibited.
- Reports must distinguish gross sales, net revenue, cost of goods sold, gross margin, shipping result, fees, taxes, adjustments, and distributable profit.
- Period attribution must follow the authoritative recognition event date and any approved accrual or deferral policy.
