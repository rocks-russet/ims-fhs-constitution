# FIN-BR-048 — Financial Governance Change and Policy Exception Control

Purpose: Ensure changes to financial rules, models, thresholds, classifications, and exceptional treatments are authorized, versioned, impact-assessed, and implemented without rewriting history.

Rules:
- Financial policy, business-rule, reference-data, ownership, distribution, approval, valuation, recognition, and posting changes must be managed as controlled governance changes.
- Every proposed change must identify:
  - change ID;
  - sponsor and owner;
  - reason and expected outcome;
  - affected rules, records, workflows, reports, and integrations;
  - financial and operational impact;
  - effective date;
  - migration or transition requirement;
  - test and validation plan;
  - approval decision;
  - rollback or contingency plan.
- A change must not apply retroactively unless retroactive treatment is explicitly approved and executed through traceable correction, migration, or restatement records.
- Historical transactions must continue to reference the rule, ownership snapshot, rate, threshold, and classification effective when the economic event was recognized, except where an approved restatement requires otherwise.
- New rule versions must be effective-dated and must not overwrite prior versions required for audit or historical calculation.
- Material changes affecting wallet balances, profit distribution, settlement, cost basis, financial rights, or reporting require documented pre-implementation reconciliation and post-implementation validation.
- Temporary exceptions must identify the exact requirement being waived, scope, reason, risk, compensating control, approver, start date, expiry date, and closure condition.
- An exception must not become a permanent operating method through repeated renewal without formal policy review.
- Exceptions must be narrowly scoped and must not waive evidence preservation, event identity, ledger traceability, access accountability, or prohibition against unauthorized alteration.
- Expired exceptions must cease automatically or be escalated before further affected activity is processed.
- Emergency changes may be implemented before ordinary approval only when necessary to protect funds, preserve evidence, or restore critical operation; they require retrospective review and formalization within the defined period.
- Change implementation must include communication to affected operators and update of relevant documentation, reference data, validation rules, and monitoring.
- Failed or partially implemented changes must be contained, reconciled, and either rolled back or completed through an approved recovery plan.
- Governance records, approvals, tests, migrations, exceptions, and validation evidence must be retained as part of the financial audit trail.
