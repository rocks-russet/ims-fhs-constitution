# FIN-BR-029 — Accrual, Deferral, and Period Attribution

Purpose: Assign financial effects to the correct reporting period when cash movement and economic recognition occur at different times.

Rules:
- Cash receipt or payment date must remain distinct from the effective economic period of revenue, expense, obligation, or entitlement.
- An accrual or deferral must identify:
  - record ID;
  - financial category;
  - amount and currency;
  - source reference;
  - covered period;
  - recognition basis;
  - reversal or release method;
  - preparer, reviewer, and approver;
  - status.
- Accrued expenses require evidence that an obligation relates to the covered period even when payment or final invoice has not occurred.
- Accrued revenue or receivable treatment requires an approved basis and must not anticipate uncompleted sales contrary to settlement rules.
- Prepaid or deferred amounts must be released only as the related goods, services, rights, or obligations are recognized.
- Period attribution must not change the immutable date or evidence of the original cash transaction.
- Recurring release schedules must be deterministic, reviewable, and linked to the originating deferred record.
- Estimates must state their calculation basis, material assumptions, and responsible approver.
- Replacement of an estimate with an authoritative amount must preserve both the original estimate and the adjustment.
- Automatic reversal entries must reference the originating accrual and must not be duplicated when the final transaction is posted.
- Period close must identify unresolved accruals, deferrals, and estimates that remain active after the reporting date.
- Corrections to a closed period must follow the active period-close and adjustment policy and remain visible in audit history.
