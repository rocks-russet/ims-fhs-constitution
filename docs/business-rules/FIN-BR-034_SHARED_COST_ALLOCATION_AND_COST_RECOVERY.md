# FIN-BR-034 — Shared Cost Allocation and Cost Recovery

Purpose: Ensure costs benefiting multiple inventories, orders, wallets, owners, or operating activities are allocated consistently, transparently, and without creating artificial profit or hidden subsidy.

Rules:
- A shared cost is any valid business cost that cannot be attributed exclusively to one inventory record, order, wallet, owner, buyer, vendor, shipment, or operating activity at initial recognition.
- Shared costs must be recorded first against an identifiable source event and must not be distributed through undocumented manual balance changes.
- Every shared-cost allocation must identify:
  - source cost record;
  - total amount and currency;
  - affected objects or beneficiaries;
  - allocation basis;
  - allocated amount per object;
  - allocation date;
  - responsible actor;
  - approval when required.
- The allocation basis must be reasonable, reproducible, and appropriate to the economic substance of the cost.
- Permitted allocation bases may include quantity, weight, acquisition value, selling value, shipment participation, usage, time, equal share, or another approved measurable driver.
- An allocation basis must not be selected solely to shift cost away from one wallet, owner, order, or reporting period.
- The total allocated amount must equal the original shared cost, subject only to documented rounding treatment.
- Rounding differences must be assigned using a deterministic rule and remain separately identifiable when material.
- Allocation to inventory cost must occur only when the cost is directly attributable to bringing the inventory to its recognized condition and location.
- Costs that do not qualify as inventory cost must remain classified as operating expense, reimbursement, receivable, payable, advance, or another appropriate financial category.
- Cost recovery from another wallet, owner, buyer, vendor, carrier, marketplace, or third party must be recorded separately from the original cost and must not erase the original expense or allocation history.
- A recovery must reference the cost being recovered and distinguish principal recovery from fees, penalties, discounts, compensation, or profit.
- Partial recovery must preserve the unrecovered balance and must not close the source cost prematurely.
- Reallocation after posting requires a linked adjustment or reversal that preserves the original allocation, reason, actor, timestamp, and approval trail.
- Shared-cost allocations and recoveries must remain auditable from the original evidence through every affected ledger and business record.
