# FIN-BR-039 — Inventory Valuation, Cost Basis, and Impairment

Purpose: Govern the financial value assigned to inventory so capital, cost of goods sold, profit, loss, and owner rights remain consistent with authoritative acquisition and transformation history.

Rules:
- Every financially recognized inventory unit or quantity must have a traceable cost basis derived from authoritative acquisition, allocation, transformation, or opening-balance events.
- Inventory cost may include only amounts permitted by the active cost policy and directly attributable to bringing the inventory to its recognized condition and location.
- Cost basis must separately preserve, where applicable:
  - purchase price;
  - allocated shipping;
  - allocated discount;
  - allocated tax or import charge;
  - directly attributable handling or preparation cost;
  - transformation allocation;
  - prior impairment or recovery;
  - currency and exchange-rate reference.
- Market price observations must not automatically overwrite historical cost basis.
- Unrealized market appreciation must not create wallet balance, distributable profit, or capital recovery rights unless an explicit approved accounting policy permits recognition.
- Inventory must be reviewed for impairment when damage, loss, obsolescence, authenticity concern, market decline, legal restriction, incomplete condition, or other evidence indicates that recorded cost may not be recoverable.
- An impairment event must identify the affected inventory, prior carrying amount, revised carrying amount, valuation basis, evidence, responsible wallet or ownership allocation, actor, approval, and timestamp.
- Impairment must not alter physical quantity, custody, ownership history, or inventory identity.
- Recovery of a prior impairment may be recognized only through a separate approved event and must not exceed the amount permitted by the active policy.
- Splitting, merging, bundling, grading, repacking, or transforming inventory must allocate existing cost basis deterministically across resulting inventory records.
- The total cost basis after a transformation must equal the valid pre-transformation cost plus approved attributable transformation costs, subject only to documented rounding.
- Cost transferred to sold inventory must be removed from available inventory value exactly once.
- Cancelled or reversed sales must restore cost basis consistently with the restored inventory state and must preserve the original sale history.
- Consigned or third-party-owned inventory must not be valued as IMS FHS-owned capital unless the governing arrangement explicitly transfers that economic risk.
- Manual cost-basis changes are prohibited except through approved adjustment, migration, impairment, recovery, or correction events.
- Inventory valuation reports must distinguish historical cost, carrying amount, market observation, impairment, and ownership allocation.
