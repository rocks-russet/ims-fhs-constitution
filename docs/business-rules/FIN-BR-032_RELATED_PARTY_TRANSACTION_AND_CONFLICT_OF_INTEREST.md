# FIN-BR-032 — Related-Party Transaction and Conflict of Interest

Purpose: Require transparent and independently reviewable treatment of financial transactions involving owners, operators, approvers, or other related parties.

Rules:
- A related-party transaction is any financial arrangement in which an IMS FHS owner, operator, approver, wallet beneficiary, close associate, or controlled entity has a direct or indirect financial interest.
- Related-party status must be declared before approval, posting, settlement, reimbursement, purchase, sale, loan, transfer, or write-off when reasonably known.
- Every related-party transaction must identify:
  - involved related party;
  - nature of the relationship;
  - transaction purpose;
  - amount and currency;
  - pricing or valuation basis;
  - approving actor;
  - supporting evidence;
  - any exception from ordinary terms.
- A person with a material conflict of interest must not be the sole approver of the affected transaction.
- Transactions with related parties must use documented terms that are commercially reasonable or explicitly approved as an owner decision.
- Preferential pricing, waived fees, delayed settlement, unusual credit, or non-standard allocation must be disclosed and approved.
- A related-party transaction must not be disguised through another wallet, contact, vendor, buyer, Settlement Account, or intermediary.
- Wallet ownership and profit rights must not be changed merely to conceal the economic beneficiary of a transaction.
- Reimbursement to an owner or operator requires the same evidence and classification standards applied to other parties.
- Related-party balances, receivables, payables, loans, and outstanding obligations must be separately identifiable in reporting.
- Corrections must preserve the original declaration, approval path, and financial history.
- Failure to disclose a known conflict must trigger review and may suspend further processing until resolved.
